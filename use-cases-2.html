Use Cases · Drug Discovery

Turning the molecular signature of exercise into medicine

A second in-silico use case for PromptGenix's Bayesian, uncertainty-aware method: reconstruct the transcriptional signature of resistance-trained human muscle, then discover the drug targets and candidate compounds that reproduce it — aimed at sarcopenia, a disease with no FDA-approved drug.

Hierarchical Bayesian Connectivity mapping Uncertainty-aware ranking
The opportunity

A disease with a market, and no drug

Age-related muscle loss (sarcopenia) is a recognized disease, yet no treatment is approved. Exercise is the only validated prescription — but many patients (bed-rest, frailty, post-surgical) cannot exercise. The platform asks a precise question: which molecular perturbations reproduce the trained-muscle state?

Why an exercise mimetic

Resistance training remodels muscle through a well-mapped program — the AMPK / SIRT1 / PGC-1α axis, mitochondrial biogenesis, and calcium handling. A drug that reproduces that transcriptional program could deliver a subset of exercise's benefit to people who cannot train.

The field's recurring failure is the "mass without strength" gap: agents that add muscle bulk without improving function. This platform designs against that from the start.

What the method returns

  • Ranked target hypotheses — genes to activate or inhibit
  • Candidate compounds — including repurposing opportunities
  • Posterior confidence with 95% credible intervals
  • Mechanism (MoA) diversity to hedge single-pathway toxicity
  • Full traceability to public data — no proprietary inputs
How it works

A five-stage discovery workflow

Signature → target → candidate → prioritized shortlist. Confidence is computed by a Bayesian engine; every stage carries its uncertainty forward rather than collapsing to a point estimate.

0
Bayesian signature
Reconstruct the exercise signature

Human muscle multi-omics is modeled with a hierarchical Bayesian mixed model (non-centered, joint cross-gene shrinkage) that absorbs the large inter-individual variance of small clinical cohorts. Output is not an up/down gene list but a per-gene posterior of the exercise effect, carried downstream as evidence w_g = E[β_g] / SD[β_g].

1
Target discovery
Find the targets that reproduce it

The signature is queried against genome-wide CRISPR knockout / overexpression perturbation profiles. A perturbation that reproduces the trained-muscle signature nominates an activation target; one that reverses it nominates an inhibition target. Network propagation extends hits to their pathway neighbors.

2
Compound + muscle-quality weighting
Rank mimetic compounds — for function, not mass

The same query runs against ~1M small-molecule transcriptional profiles (LINCS). Compounds whose effect mimics exercise become candidate mimetics — many are approved or in-clinic, i.e. repurposing opportunities with a faster regulatory path. A quality weight q_g up-weights mitochondrial and SR-Ca²⁺ genes (the machinery of force and fatigue resistance) and down-weights pure hypertrophy markers.

3
Prioritize + diversify
A shortlist you can defend

Candidates are ranked by the lower credible bound of a two-sided uncertainty score — conservative by construction — then clustered by mechanism so the shortlist spans distinct MoAs. This structurally hedges the single-pathway toxicity that sank earlier exercise-mimetic candidates.

4
Automate
Reproducible, accession-driven runs

Stages 0–3 run as one orchestrated pipeline with a fast GPU-accelerated sampler, pulling public data (MoTrPAC, GEO, LINCS) automatically. Deterministic reruns with pinned versions keep every result auditable.

The engine

Uncertainty-aware connectivity

Standard connectivity mapping collapses a noisy query and a noisy reference into one opaque score. Here, both sides keep their uncertainty, and the score itself becomes a distribution.

Probabilistic connectivity score

Each gene contributes to the connectivity score weighted by its posterior evidence and its muscle-quality weight, so uncertain and off-target genes fade automatically:

CSp = Σg wg · qg · rp,g
wg = E[βg] / SD[βg]   // posterior evidence — uncertainty-damped query weight
rank by P(CSp > c) and the 5% credible bound // not a single point estimate

Interpretability rule: a candidate is only ranked on evidence it actually carries — wide-posterior genes cannot inflate a score.

Two-sided update — CSp(s) = f( βquery(s), β̃p,ref(s) ),  s = 1…S
Monte-Carlo propagation of both the query posterior and the reference replicate variance — directly targeting the documented low reproducibility of cell-line connectivity maps.

Why it's different

Beyond off-the-shelf connectivity

Human muscle

Starts from resistance-trained human muscle — not the cancer cell lines most connectivity work relies on.

Posterior, not point

Small-n, high-variance cohorts are absorbed by partial pooling; ranking keeps the full uncertainty.

Two-sided noise

Query and reference uncertainty are propagated together, confronting CMap's known reproducibility gap.

Function endpoint

Quality weighting steers toward mitochondrial and contractile mechanism, not raw muscle mass.

Targets in scope

Known axes as a starting point

The literature axis is where the query begins — the goal is to move beyond it to novel targets and chemistry. In first real-data validation, DNAJA3, a mitochondrial co-chaperone, surfaced as a top mimicker — a coherent biological sanity signal aligned with the mitochondrial program.
