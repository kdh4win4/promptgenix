MUSS: A Multi-scale and Sequence-based Model for Single-cell Gene Regulation

2026-11-05Dohoon (Roy) Kim

Predicts cell-type-specific gene expression and enhancer-gene links from DNA sequence plus paired scATAC-seq/scRNA-seq, and claims to predict expression for genes and peaks absent from task training, which prior fixed-vocabulary single-cell models did not do.

What it changes at the bench

A computational biologist with paired multiome data could test the released code and checkpoints on enhancer-gene linking or expression prediction in their own tissue. Nothing changes for wet-lab or clinical workflows. The abstract reports no numbers, so there is no basis yet to replace an existing method.

Where this sits

  • Maturity: in silico only — nothing has been run at a bench
  • Evidence: a preprint
  • Independently verified: not established
  • Domain: omics

Limitations

Stated: none in the abstract. Unstated, and not checkable from the abstract: (1) 'strong performance' and 'achieves' are unquantified; no metrics, effect sizes, variance, or seeds are given. (2) Baselines are unnamed, so cherry-picking cannot be ruled out, and it is unclear whether the single-cell omics baselines were tuned as carefully as MUSS. (3) Train/test splits are not described. Random cell splits leak donor and batch structure; donor-, tissue- or dataset-level holdouts are needed. (4) The frozen pre-trained DNA encoder may have seen the same loci or regulatory sequences used for evaluation. (5) 'Unseen genes and peaks' may be easy if they are paralogs or share sequence with training genes; sequence identity to training data is not reported. (6) Enhancer-gene linking ground truth (e.g., eQTL, CRISPRi, Hi-C) is unspecified; if it is correlation-derived, the task is partly circular. (7) Zero-shot clustering labels and metrics are unspecified. (8) No ablation is mentioned for the peak-peak attention, the distance bias, the peak MLM pretraining, or the frozen encoder, so the contribution of each component is unknown. (9) The release is tied to a NeurIPS-track submission and has not been peer reviewed.

My take

The claim I care about here is generalisation to genes and peaks unseen during task training, because that is the one thing the fixed-vocabulary single-cell models genuinely cannot do, and it is also the claim most likely to be softer than it sounds. "Unseen" is doing a lot of work. A gene that never appeared in task training but has a paralogue that did, or shares promoter architecture with one that did, is not really unseen to a sequence model — it is interpolation wearing a holdout's clothes. The number I would want, and which is not in the abstract, is sequence identity between the held-out loci and the training set. Without it I cannot tell whether this is generalisation or near-duplicate retrieval.

The split matters just as much. I have spent years cleaning up analyses where someone split single-cell data by cell and reported a beautiful number, and the model had simply learned the donor. Cells from the same donor are not independent samples, and neither are cells from the same 10x run. Donor-level or dataset-level holdout is the only version of this that means anything, and the abstract does not say which was used.

Then enhancer-gene linking. If the ground truth is correlation-derived — peak accessibility correlated with gene expression across cells — then the model is being scored against a quantity built from the same data it was trained on, and strong performance is close to tautological. CRISPRi perturbation data is the only ground truth I would accept without argument. eQTLs I would take with caveats. Hi-C answers a different question. The abstract does not say which was used either.

None of that means the architecture is wrong. Deriving peak- and gene-level representations from a frozen pretrained sequence encoder, then integrating them through peak-peak self-attention and distance-biased peak-gene interactions, is a sensible design, and the frozen encoder is a reasonable way to buy sequence representation cheaply. What the abstract does not do is report a single number: "strong performance against sequence-based and single-cell omics baselines" names no metric, no variance, and no baseline. For a paper accepted to NeurIPS 2026 that is a choice about the abstract, not necessarily about the work.

Code, datasets and model checkpoints are released, which is more than most, and which makes the honest move obvious: run it on your own multiome with a donor-held-out split and find out. That is what I intend to do before I cite it.
