Predicting protein structures at any folding stage from sequence

2026-11-03Dohoon (Roy) Kim

FoldScape generates Cα ensembles at a requested folding fraction Q and covers 0.722 of that coordinate against 0.05 for seed-varied AlphaFold 3. The tension is the baseline: AF3 with varied seeds was never a folding sampler, and the paper reports no reconstruction accuracy, no state populations, and no comparison against the model doing the same thing three months earlier.

What it changes at the bench

A bioinformatician could in principle request ensembles at chosen folding fractions for a given sequence without running per-protein MD, which might help generate hypotheses about intermediates or partially unfolded regions. Nothing changes for drug discovery yet: there is no experimental or downstream application shown, and the outputs have not been checked against folding or unfolding data.

Where this sits

  • Maturity: in silico only — nothing has been run at a bench
  • Evidence: a preprint
  • Independently verified: no
  • Domain: protein

Limitations

Stated: AF3 is better near the native state, so the methods are complementary, not a replacement. Unstated or unclear: (1) 'order of magnitude more folding coordinate' depends on authors' own order parameters and binning, and seed-varied AF3 is not designed to sample folding stages, so the comparison is somewhat apples-to-oranges; (2) the accuracy metric behind 'approximately constant along Q' is not defined in the abstract; (3) n=20 human proteins, 36-857 residues, all selected by the authors; (4) no physical validation against experimental observables (HDX, smFRET, phi-values, SAXS) and no comparison against MD or coarse-grained baselines; (5) possible leakage: test proteins or homologues may appear in the coarse-grained or mechanical-unfolding training trajectories, and this is not addressed; (6) the crop-and-superpose extension to longer chains has no reported accuracy; (7) pulling-velocity reproducibility shows internal consistency, not correctness; (8) no ablations for the rectified-flow decoder or the hierarchical sliding-window scheme; (9) the 'without per-protein simulation' framing omits that training needs large simulation datasets; (10) no code or data availability statement is visible in the abstract.

My take

Coverage is the wrong number to lead with.

FoldScape reports median coverage of 0.722 along Q against 0.05 for seed-varied AlphaFold 3. But seed-varied AF3 is not a folding sampler, and that was settled before this paper. Outeiral, Nissley and Deane (Bioinformatics 2022) scored structure predictors against HDX-characterised intermediates for 11 proteins and folding kinetics for 170, and found chain length alone outperformed every predictor at classifying two-state versus multistate folding. Beating that fourteenfold is beating a tool at a job it was never doing.

The missing number sits right beside the one being advertised. Reconstruction accuracy "remains approximately stable across the evaluated stages" — no Cα-RMSD, no lDDT, no value at all — and no population is given for any state. An ensemble that spans Q evenly tells you what is reachable, not what is occupied, and those are different claims. The public bar is in free energies, not coverage: BioEmu (Science 2025) reports mean absolute error under 0.9 kcal/mol against MEGAscale, 0.74 kcal/mol across 12 DESRES fast folders, and 0.9 kcal/mol on 17 held-out CATH systems.

The absent baseline is specific, and it is three months old. PathFold (bioRxiv, August 2026) is the same shape — Cα-level, sequence alone, trained on 940 proteins and 728,597 reversed steered-MD frames — and it scores itself against held-out SMD with alignment scores of 0.80 to 0.85 against a measured replicate ceiling of 0.894. That ceiling is the honest move: five independent SMD runs on the same protein agree only to 0.894, so no model trained on that data should claim more. FoldScape reports no equivalent.

Which raises the thing both papers inherit. PathFold says outright that its reversed unfolding trajectories "do not represent genuine folding dynamics." FoldScape trains on mechanical-unfolding trajectories too, and pulling a chain apart from its termini is not folding run backwards — the order in which structure comes apart under force is set by where the force is applied. "Reproducible across pulling velocities" shows the method is stable; it does not show the pathway is the folding pathway.

Cα-only means no side chains, so no pockets, so nothing here reaches drug discovery yet. Φ-values on ubiquitin or CspB would move me. Coverage will not.
