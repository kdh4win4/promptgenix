Static Recovery Is Not Dynamic Stability: Dynamics-Aware Benchmarking of Protein Motif Scaffolding

2026-11-05Dohoon (Roy) Kim

Scores motif-scaffolding outputs on whether the designed scaffold and motif remain stable under all-atom molecular dynamics relative to reference simulations, a check that static refolding cannot provide.

What it changes at the bench

A computational biologist running motif scaffolding should not treat static refolding success as evidence of full-scaffold dynamical fidelity. An MD screen could be added before committing designs to synthesis, at substantial compute cost. Compact, self-contained motifs pass MD more often than interface-like or context-dependent ones, which tells you where static pass rates are least trustworthy. Nothing changes at the wet-lab level yet.

Where this sits

  • Maturity: in silico only — nothing has been run at a bench
  • Evidence: a preprint
  • Independently verified: no
  • Domain: protein

Limitations

Stated: static refolding does not establish dynamical fidelity; compact motifs pass more often than interface-like ones. Not addressed or not visible in the abstract: (1) The MD analysis covers only the 55 model-problem pairs from the original MotifBench benchmark that already passed static solving — not the 220-case BILBY-PRO benchmark the same paper introduces, so it is conditional on a selection step and says nothing about designs that failed statically. (2) The MD-support threshold and the 'pass all checks' criterion are authors' constructs with no external calibration against experimental stability or folding data. (3) Simulation details are missing: force field, water model, simulation length, replicate count, how reference ensembles were generated, and the convergence of free-energy landscapes. (4) No uncertainty estimates or statistical tests are given for the compact-versus-interface claim, and that claim is correlational with motif type confounded with size and contact count. (5) The six models are only partly named in the abstract, and the coverage and unique-yield numbers are on BILBY-PRO, which the authors built themselves. (6) Possible training-data overlap between generative models and PROSITE-linked PDB structures is not discussed. (7) No wet-lab validation of any MD-flagged or MD-failed design. (8) Comparison to MotifBench's own success criteria is not given in the abstract, so it is unclear how much the MD layer changes model rankings.

My take

This is the kind of paper I think the field needs more of, and almost nobody gets credit for writing: it argues that the metric everyone reports is measuring the wrong thing. Motif scaffolding is scored on whether a structure predictor refolds the design back to the intended motif. That is a static check, and a design can pass it while the scaffold comes apart the moment you give it thermal energy. The paper puts it precisely: static refolding success "provides useful evidence of local motif recovery but does not establish dynamical fidelity of the full scaffold."

The numbers are the part worth carrying away. BILBY-MD, their reference-based all-atom molecular dynamics framework, was applied to statically solved designs from the original MotifBench benchmark — not to BILBY-PRO, the 220-case benchmark the same paper introduces, which is worth keeping straight. Of 55 statically solved model-problem pairs, 31 satisfy their MD-supported criterion and only 8 pass all checks. Eight out of fifty-five. If you have been reading static pass rates as a yield estimate, that is the correction.

The second finding is the one I would actually use: compact, self-contained motifs are often MD-supported, whereas interface-like or context-dependent motifs fail more frequently. That tells you where the standard pass rate is least trustworthy, and it maps onto intuition — a motif whose conformation is held in place by a binding partner has no reason to hold that conformation alone. If you are scaffolding an interface epitope, static success should not reassure you. I spent a decade on HIV immunogen work where exactly that distinction was the problem, so this one lands for me. The paper reports the direction without quantifying the gap, so I would not put a number on it.

Where I would push back: molecular dynamics is also a proxy. BILBY-MD is explicitly reference-based — it compares motif drift, scaffold drift, contact preservation and dihedral free-energy landscapes against reference simulations, not against measured stability. The pass criterion is the authors' own construct. So the paper replaces one uncalibrated criterion with a better-motivated but still uncalibrated one, and the case that it tracks reality rests on physical plausibility rather than on experiment. That is a reasonable argument. It is not yet a settled one.

The conditioning also bounds what can be concluded. Only designs that already passed static solving entered the MD analysis, so this says nothing about designs that static scoring wrongly rejected — which, if you care about yield rather than precision, is the number you actually need.

Accepted at NeurIPS 2026 in the Evaluations and Datasets track, with the dataset and the MD code both released. The narrow, cheap version of the lesson: before you commit a scaffolded interface motif to synthesis, simulate it. The compute is real, and it is less than the synthesis.
