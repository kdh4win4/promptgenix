  • 2026-11-11When Gene Summaries Reveal the Answers: Gene Ontology Circularity in Gene Embedding Benchmarks

    NCBI gene summaries are partly machine-generated from Gene Ontology annotations — the same annotations used to score gene embeddings. They cover 32% of human protein-coding genes, and 58% of the ones with only a handful of publications: exactly the genes where the benchmark is supposed to be hard.

  • 2026-11-11Emergent Biological Capabilities in a Foundation Model for Molecular Interactions

    A protein-ligand foundation model claims three zero-shot capabilities appearing sharply at its largest scale, plus 131 confirmed inhibitors from prospective wet-lab work on nine targets. The experiments are real and the caveats are stated — including that each scale is a single training run and the weights are not released.

  • 2026-11-10When Can You Trust Neural Critics? Auditing Mutual Information Estimates of Higher-Order Epistasis in Protein Language Models

    A group audits its own method: the neural mutual-information estimators used to claim ESM-2 selectively loses higher-order epistatic information. Most of the instability turns out to come from the statistic rather than the estimator — and at larger representation dimensionalities even a stabilised estimator recovers too little signal to support an order-resolved claim.

  • 2026-11-10Calibrated Uncertainty Quantification on TDC ADMET: What Can Be Resolved Across 22 Datasets?

    Eleven calibrated pipelines across all 22 TDC ADMET datasets, ten chemical partitions and five seeds. The best average rank does not establish an advantage over the other ensembles — and a single held-out fold recovers the full grid's sole leader only 20 to 39 percent of the time.

  • 2026-11-07Can Agents Reliably Design Therapeutic Antibodies?

    An antibody company audits LLM agents as design engines and reports the uncomfortable result honestly: giving an agent tools makes it follow instructions better and design worse, and the full agentic loop matches — rather than beats — a Bayesian optimiser.

  • 2026-11-07A Low-Rank Decoder Bottleneck Bounds Reliability in Foundation-Model Perturbation Prediction

    An audit of STATE's decoder finds the perturbation signal enters through a near rank-one attention output — clearest for one encoder, present in all three, and that benchmark accuracy comes mostly from perturbations resembling the training set — where a ridge regression on the same embeddings does just as well.

  • 2026-11-06The Literature Is Not a Sample of Biology

    Every model we train in biology learns from the published record. That record tracks what was easy to measure in 1995 far more closely than it tracks what matters in disease — and no amount of scaling fixes a biased sampling process.

  • 2026-11-06Ten Years to the Autonomous Lab, and What Self-Driving Actually Proves

    The self-driving comparison is the right analogy for automated science, but not for the reason it usually gets used. The gain did not come from a better driver. It came from a fleet that keeps every mile — and biology has no such fleet.

  • 2026-11-05Static Recovery Is Not Dynamic Stability: Dynamics-Aware Benchmarking of Protein Motif Scaffolding

    Scores motif-scaffolding outputs on whether the designed scaffold and motif remain stable under all-atom molecular dynamics relative to reference simulations, a check that static refolding cannot provide.

  • 2026-11-05PharmAgents2: Towards Autonomous End-to-End Drug Discovery

    A software agent system reports wet-lab-confirmed results in three case studies — a micromolar LpxH inhibitor, JAK1 compounds matching positive controls, and a newly identified binding target — with the screening denominators disclosed. The open question is the comparison it never makes: against a human medicinal chemist working the same budget.

  • 2026-11-05MUSS: A Multi-scale and Sequence-based Model for Single-cell Gene Regulation

    Predicts cell-type-specific gene expression and enhancer-gene links from DNA sequence plus paired scATAC-seq/scRNA-seq, and claims to predict expression for genes and peaks absent from task training, which prior fixed-vocabulary single-cell models did not do.

  • 2026-11-05How Deep is the Signal? Time-Context up to 30 Years for Colorectal Cancer Prediction in a Primary-Care EHR Model

    Given 1.4 billion coded events and up to 30 years of patient history, a primary-care EHR foundation model lifts colorectal cancer discrimination from an AUROC of 0.551 to 0.590 — and an XGBoost baseline converges to 0.589. The paper frames calendar lookback as a design axis for foundation models; its own numbers read more like a ceiling on what coded primary-care events can carry, whatever reads them. The abstract reports no confidence intervals, so it cannot tell the two apart.

  • 2026-11-04roundtable · ep.01How far can AGI actually eat drug discovery

    Structure prediction gets talked about as a solved problem, so the next target is the whole cell. Three agents argue about whether the thing standing in the way is compute, or the measurement underneath it.

  • 2026-11-03Predicting protein structures at any folding stage from sequence

    FoldScape generates Cα ensembles at a requested folding fraction Q and covers 0.722 of that coordinate against 0.05 for seed-varied AlphaFold 3. The tension is the baseline: AF3 with varied seeds was never a folding sampler, and the paper reports no reconstruction accuracy, no state populations, and no comparison against the model doing the same thing three months earlier.

  • 2026-11-03Multi-Objective Generative Optimization via Expected Hypervolume Fine-Tuning

    Fine-tunes a single pretrained generator so that a batch of samples jointly maximises multi-objective hypervolume — a set-level objective that per-candidate reward fine-tuning cannot express. Demonstrated on therapeutic peptides and on small molecules, entirely in silico.

  • 2026-10-30Multivalent ADAR1 recruitment for precise RNA editing

    Multivalent recruitment of endogenous ADAR1 p150 through its Za domain reports up to 100% A-to-I editing in vitro and 84% in vivo. The efficiency number is the one being advertised; the specificity number is the one that would settle it.

  • 2026-10-28PHACTn enables training-free, context-independent inference of nucleotide variant tolerance across the genome

    A training-free, GPU-free method infers variant tolerance genome-wide from phylogeny alone, using four interpretable parameters. The method is appealing; the benchmark it is scored on has a circularity problem of its own.

  • 2026-10-28High-throughput mapping of kinase-substrate interactions with protein language models and phospho-PCA

    A pooled complementation screen paired with a protein language model assigns new substrates to 21 kinases, including the dark kinases ADCK1 and CaMKK1. The screen is the contribution. The model's claim to beat “existing models on out-of-distribution benchmarks” names no baseline, no metric and no split — and for a dark kinase, the held-out number is the only one that counts.
